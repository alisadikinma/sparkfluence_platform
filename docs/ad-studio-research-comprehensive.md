## Part 1: Advertising Psychology Foundations

### Cialdini's Six Principles—Parameterized for Video Ads

**Reciprocity** works by giving value before asking. Lead with free tips, templates, or insights before introducing the product. Scripts should open with "Before I show you [product], here's [valuable insight]..." or "I'm giving away something most companies charge $500 for..." This principle performs best for lead generation, educational products, and SaaS trials targeting cold audiences.

**Commitment/Consistency** leverages identity alignment. Get viewers to self-identify early with phrases like "If you're someone who..." or "You're here because you're serious about..." This triggers psychological follow-through. The principle works powerfully for high-ticket items, subscription services, and products requiring behavior change.

**Social Proof** addresses uncertainty through crowd validation. Effective scripts incorporate "Join 50,000+ people who've already..." or "The #1 rated [product] in [category]..." Display user counts, ratings, and testimonials prominently. Use this for new brands, complex purchases, and competitive markets—avoid when numbers are unimpressive or positioning is exclusive.

**Authority** transfers credibility from experts. Scripts should reference credentials: "Developed by doctors at [institution]" or "Featured in Forbes, The New York Times..." This principle is essential for health products, financial services, and B2B—but can backfire with anti-establishment audiences.

**Liking** increases persuasion through relatability. Match presenter demographics to target audience and use language like "I was exactly where you are..." or "As a fellow [parent/entrepreneur]..." Critical for personal brands, lifestyle products, and direct-to-consumer categories.

**Scarcity** creates urgency through limited availability. Use specific language: "Only 47 spots remaining" or "This offer disappears in 24 hours." Deploy for product launches and promotions but only when scarcity is genuine—false scarcity destroys trust.

### Cognitive Biases for Conversion

**Loss aversion** drives action through fear of missing out—losses feel 2x more intense than equivalent gains. Frame messaging around what viewers lose by not acting: "Stop losing $X every month to [problem]" or "Every day you wait costs you..." Most effective for insurance, security, and problem-solving products.

**Anchoring** sets price perception through first numbers presented. Always show competitor/original price before your offer: "Most programs cost $2,000+. Today, you're getting it for $297." Use precise numbers for stronger anchoring effect.

**Framing** determines how identical information lands. "90% success rate" outperforms "10% failure rate." Structure scripts with negative problem framing transitioning to positive solution framing—this combination maximizes emotional impact.

**Bandwagon effect** creates momentum through crowd behavior. Scripts should emphasize adoption velocity: "Thousands are already switching..." or "This is what everyone's talking about..." Pair with FOMO language for maximum impact.

### Emotional vs. Rational Appeals Matrix

Research from the IPA DataBANK across 1,400+ campaigns shows purely emotional campaigns generate **2x higher profitability** than rational approaches (31% vs. 16% lift). However, optimal strategy depends on context:

| Context | Primary Appeal | Secondary Appeal |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| B2C lifestyle/luxury | Emotional (80%) | Rational (20%) |
| B2C technical products | Rational (60%) | Emotional (40%) |
| B2B enterprise | Rational (70%) | Emotional (30%) |
| B2B startup/tech | Emotional (50%) | Rational (50%) |

**Core emotional triggers** for scripts: Fear/FOMO ("Don't miss out"), Joy ("Imagine the feeling when..."), Belonging ("Join the tribe"), Status ("For those who expect more"), Anger/Frustration ("Sick and tired of...?"), Hope ("Finally achieve...").

**Optimal structure for blending both**: Open with emotional hook (0-5 seconds) → Agitate emotional pain (5-15 seconds) → Bridge to rational benefits (15-25 seconds) → Provide rational proof (25-40 seconds) → Close with emotional urgency (final seconds).

### Attention Economics Parameters

Average attention spans and hook windows vary significantly by generation and platform. The critical finding: **45-65% of viewers decide within 3 seconds** whether to continue watching.

| Generation | Hook Window | Decision Point |
|------------|-------------|----------------|
| Gen Z | 1.3 seconds | Immediate |
| Millennials | 3-5 seconds | 5 seconds |
| Gen X | 5-8 seconds | 10 seconds |
| Boomers | 8-12 seconds | 15 seconds |

**Pattern interrupt techniques** that trigger the brain's reticular activating system: Visual jolts (color shifts, dramatic zooms), Audio cues (sudden sounds, tempo shifts), Cognitive dissonance ("Wait, what?"), Format breaks (fourth wall, glitches), Time manipulation (slow motion, speed ramping).

## Part 2: Video Ad Frameworks with Timing Parameters

### AIDA Framework (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

| Phase | Timing % | 30-sec | 60-sec | Function |
|-------|----------|--------|--------|----------|
| Attention | 10-15% | 3-5s | 6-9s | Pattern interrupt hook |
| Interest | 25-30% | 7-9s | 15-18s | Problem resonance |
| Desire | 35-40% | 10-12s | 21-24s | Benefit showcase |
| Action | 15-20% | 5-6s | 9-12s | Clear CTA |

**Best for**: Product launches, brand awareness, e-commerce, complex products requiring explanation. **Avoid for**: Ultra-short formats under 15 seconds.

### PAS Framework (Problem, Agitate, Solution)

| Phase | Timing % | 30-sec | 60-sec | Function |
|-------|----------|--------|--------|----------|
| Problem | 20-25% | 6-8s | 12-15s | Pain identification |
| Agitate | 30-35% | 9-11s | 18-21s | Amplify discomfort |
| Solution | 40-50% | 12-15s | 24-30s | Product + CTA |

**Agitation techniques**: Consequences method ("If you don't fix this, [worse outcome]..."), Escalation ("It starts with [small issue], then becomes [bigger issue]..."), Negative future pacing ("Imagine 6 months from now, still dealing with...").

**Best for**: Problem-aware audiences, B2B solutions, health/wellness, pain-driven purchases. **Avoid for**: Pure brand/lifestyle content.

### BAB Framework (Before, After, Bridge)

| Phase | Timing % | 30-sec | 60-sec | Function |
|-------|----------|--------|--------|----------|
| Before | 25-30% | 8-9s | 15-18s | Current painful state |
| After | 35-40% | 10-12s | 21-24s | Desired future |
| Bridge | 30-35% | 9-11s | 18-21s | Product as path + CTA |

**Best for**: Transformation products, fitness/weight loss, personal development, career/education. **Avoid for**: Technical products where transformation is less visual.

### Hook-Story-Offer Framework

| Phase | Timing % | 30-sec | 60-sec | Function |
|-------|----------|--------|--------|----------|
| Hook | 10-15% | 3-5s | 6-9s | Stop scroll |
| Story | 50-60% | 15-18s | 30-36s | Build connection |
| Offer | 25-30% | 8-10s | 15-18s | Value proposition + CTA |

**Best for**: Direct response, social media ads, funnel-based businesses, info products. **Avoid for**: B2B enterprise, corporate contexts.

### Framework Selection Logic

```
IF campaign_goal = "awareness" AND audience_temp = "cold":
    THEN use AIDA or 3-Act
    
IF campaign_goal = "conversion" AND audience_temp = "problem-aware":
    THEN use PAS
    
IF product_type = "transformation-based":
    THEN use BAB
    
IF platform = "TikTok" OR platform = "Reels":
    THEN use Hook-Story-Offer
    
IF b2b = true AND formal_industry = true:
    THEN use AIDA with extended timing
```

## Part 3: Platform-Specific Parameters

### TikTok Configuration

**Technical specs**: 9:16 vertical mandatory, 1080×1920px resolution, 21-34 seconds optimal for conversions (280% more conversions than other lengths).

**Content requirements**: Phone-shot aesthetic preferred, native TikTok fonts/effects, trending sounds critical (88% say sound is essential), visual variety every 2-3 seconds, text overlays at 5-10 words per second.

**Hook characteristics**: First 6 seconds make or break, "POV:" format, comment-response style, "They don't want you to know..." formula, face-first opens outperform product shots.

**Algorithmic signals**: Completion rates heavily weighted, trending sound usage, native editing features, creator authenticity over polish.

### Instagram Reels Configuration

**Differentiation from TikTok**: Slightly more polished acceptable, older audience (28-43 median), higher purchase intent, stronger shopping integration, avoid TikTok watermarks (algorithm deprioritizes).

**Content approach**: Visual quality matters more, brand-forward acceptable, lifestyle integration, clean editing with fewer chaotic transitions.

**Shopping integration**: Product tags enable one-tap purchase, "Shop Now" buttons, catalog sync for dynamic ads, AR try-on capabilities.

### Facebook/Meta Configuration

**Feed specs**: 4:5 ratio recommended (claims more screen space), 15-60 seconds optimal, sound-off default (85% muted)—captions mandatory.

**Content that converts**: UGC-style outperforms polished production, problem-solution format, real testimonials with faces/voices, before/after transformations.

**Placement optimization**: Feed (4:5, product-focused), Reels (9:16, fast-paced, trend-aware), Stories (9:16, quick, time-sensitive, personal).

### LinkedIn Configuration

**B2B requirements**: Professional tone but increasingly conversational, thought leadership integration, decision-maker targeting through job title/function, 80% watch muted—captions essential.

**Optimal lengths**: Awareness 15-30s, Consideration 30-90s, thought leadership 60-120s.

**High-performing content types**: How-to tutorials, industry insights with data, customer success stories, behind-the-scenes authenticity, problem identification ("3 mistakes companies make...").

### YouTube Configuration

**Skippable pre-roll**: First 5 seconds critical—skip button appears, hook must land immediately, include brand early (viewers may skip), 30 seconds to 2 minutes optimal.

**Bumper ads (6 seconds)**: Single message only, visual-first design, bold imagery, no builds—hit message immediately, designed for frequency.

**Shorts**: Vertical 9:16 mandatory, sound-on default, 15-30 seconds sweet spot, match organic Shorts aesthetic, creator-style content.

### Cross-Platform Sound Strategy

| Platform | Default | Strategy |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Facebook Feed | Muted | Design for sound-off, captions essential |
| TikTok | On | Sound-on critical (93%), trending audio |
| LinkedIn | Muted | Captions essential, professional VO |
| YouTube | On | Audio integral to experience |
| Instagram Reels | Variable | Trending audio boosts discovery |

## Part 4: Age-Based Audience Parameters

### Gen Z (18-24) Configuration

**Attention parameters**: 1.3-second hook window, 15-25+ cuts per minute, 2-4 seconds per shot maximum, 15-30 second optimal length.

**Authenticity requirements**: Lo-fi production preferred, smartphone-shot aesthetic, real people (not models), embrace imperfection, meme-native content, anti-marketing energy.

**Humor calibration**: Absurdism (Duolingo owl style), meta-irony, self-deprecation, nihilistic undertones acceptable, avoid "How do you do, fellow kids" energy.

**Trust drivers**: UGC and creator endorsement (not influencer—there's a difference), authenticity over polish, community-driven validation.

**Script language**: Casual/peer-to-peer, current internet slang (must be accurate), direct address, self-aware/ironic voice, avoid corporate buzzwords entirely.

**Primary avoid list**: Logo in first 3 seconds, slow introductions, polished/cinematic production, scripted-feeling delivery, outdated meme references.

### Millennials (25-39) Configuration

**Attention parameters**: 3-5 second hook window, 10-15 cuts per minute, 4-6 seconds per shot, 30-60 second optimal length.

**Values integration**: Environmental/social benefit messaging, cause alignment (87% want purchases with positive impact), brand transparency, experience-over-possessions framing.

**Nostalgia triggers**: 1990s-2000s references, VHS/early internet aesthetics, childhood callbacks—61% respond more positively to nostalgic advertising.

**Trust drivers**: Peer reviews, values alignment proof, consistent brand experience, authentic (not influencer-heavy) endorsements.

**Script language**: Authentic, witty, values-forward, conversational but not juvenile, cultural references welcome.

**Primary avoid list**: Shallow/transactional messaging, ignoring social/environmental values, surprising/shocking ads (only 4% respond positively).

### Gen X (40-54) Configuration

**Attention parameters**: 5-8 second hook window, 6-12 cuts per minute, 5-10 seconds per shot, 30-90 second optimal length.

**Skepticism management**: 54% feel ignored by brands, highly tuned BS detectors, no gimmicks or surface-level promises, straightforward messaging only.

**Quality/value focus**: Best deals emphasized, durability and long-term value, testimonials highly effective, in-depth information valued.

**Trust drivers**: Real customer testimonials, management responses to reviews, consistent brand experience, quick customer service.

**Script language**: Direct, no-nonsense, practical, solution-oriented, respectful of intelligence, no slang or trendy jargon.

**Primary avoid list**: Trying to be "cool" or trendy, flashy high-energy editing, vague/exaggerated claims, influencer-heavy content (54% skeptical).

### Boomers (55+) Configuration

**Attention parameters**: 8-12 second hook window, 4-8 cuts per minute, 10-15+ seconds per shot acceptable, 60-120 second optimal length.

**Trust requirements**: Brand heritage emphasized, professional credentials, years in business, human customer service options, traditional authority signals over influencers.

**Clarity preferences**: Jargon-free language, larger readable text, step-by-step information, thorough explanations, key point reinforcement acceptable.

**Script language**: Clear, respectful, professional, benefit-focused, educational tone, direct honest communication.

**Primary avoid list**: Fast-paced editing, slang/internet language, influencer-style content, stereotyping/condescending portrayals, small hard-to-read text.

### Generational Comparison Matrix

## Part 5: B2B vs. B2C Parameters

### Decision Cycle Adaptation

**B2B context**: Average buying cycle 6-18 months with 6-10 stakeholders involved. Content must map to buyer journey—TOFU (thought leadership, 15-30s), MOFU (case studies, 30-90s), BOFU (detailed demos, 5-30 minutes).

**B2C context**: Decisions happen in minutes to hours. Short-form video drives 80% of impulse purchases in some contexts. Content optimized for immediate action.

### Appeal Calibration

| Factor | B2B Setting | B2C Setting |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Primary driver | Logic + risk reduction | Emotion + urgency |
| Proof type | Case studies, ROI data | Reviews, social proof |
| CTA style | Research-oriented | Action-oriented |
| Video length | 2-10 min acceptable | Under 1 min preferred |
| Tone | Professional-conversational | Casual-exciting |

### B2B Script Requirements

**Stakeholder messaging matrix**: C-suite (ROI, strategic value), Technical evaluators (features, integration, security), End users (workflow, ease of use), Finance (cost analysis, timeline).

**Authority building elements**: Named client testimonials, analyst validation, measurable case study results (e.g., "43% pipeline increase"), certifications, data-backed claims.

**LinkedIn optimization**: 4:5 or 1:1 aspect ratio for mobile, subtitles mandatory, thought leader ads for authenticity, retargeting (influences 53% of SQLs).

**CTA progression**: Awareness ("Learn More," "Watch Demo") → Consideration ("Download Guide," "Join Webinar") → Decision ("Request Demo," "Start Free Trial").

### B2C Script Requirements

**Emotional triggers**: Happiness/joy (74% with positive emotional response want to buy), FOMO (60% purchase due to FOMO within 24 hours), belonging, aspiration, nostalgia.

**Social proof implementation**: Real-time activity ("9,300 people bought this"), UGC unboxing/reviews, influencer endorsement (71% more likely to buy based on social referrals), purchase count indicators.

**Urgency tactics**: Countdown timers (14% CTR increase), limited-time offers, low stock alerts ("Only 2 left"), flash sales, exclusive access framing.

**CTA language**: Urgency-driven ("Shop Now," "Buy Today," "Limited Time"), action-oriented ("Get Yours," "Claim Your Discount"), value-explicit ("Save 50% Now").

### Messaging Framework Differences

| Element | B2B Framing | B2C Framing |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Pain point | "Losing deals due to slow response times" | "Tired of bad skin days?" |
| Stakes | Revenue, efficiency, competitive advantage | Time, money, self-image |
| Solution | Business outcomes, ROI demonstration | Personal transformation, immediate benefits |
| Proof | Case studies with metrics, named clients | Reviews/ratings, before/after |
| CTA | "See how it works" | "Shop now—20% off" |

## Part 6: CTA & Conversion Optimization

### High-Converting CTA Formulas

**Core structure**: `[Action Verb] + [Your/My] + [Benefit/Outcome] + [Now/Today]`

**Critical finding**: First-person CTAs ("Get my free guide") generate **90% higher CTR** than second-person ("Get your free guide").

**Power verbs by tier**: Tier 1 (Get, Start, Discover, Claim, Try, Join), Tier 2 (Shop, Learn, Explore, Create, Save).

### CTA Templates by Objective

| Objective | CTA Templates |
|-----------|---------------|
| Lead gen | "Get Your Free [Resource]," "Download the [Item]," "Unlock [Benefit]" |
| Purchase | "Shop Now," "Add to Cart—Save [%]," "Claim Your [Offer]" |
| Trial | "Start Free for [X] Days," "Try Risk-Free," "No Credit Card Required" |
| Sign-up | "Join [X]+ [Audience]," "Create Your Free Account" |

### Psychology Tactics

**Loss aversion CTAs**: "Don't miss out on [benefit]," "Stop losing [X] to [problem]," "Lock in [benefit] before prices increase."

**Risk reversal phrases**: "No credit card required," "Cancel anytime," "30-day money-back guarantee," "100% risk-free."

**Curiosity gap**: "Discover the secret to [outcome]," "See what's inside," "Find out why [surprising fact]."

### Urgency & Scarcity (Ethical Application)

**Effective urgency language**: "Today only," "Ends [specific date]," "Last chance," "While supplies last."

**Authenticity rule**: Specific outperforms vague. "Only 4 left" beats "A few left." "Offer ends Friday at midnight" beats "Sale ending soon."

**Ethical boundaries**: Never use fake countdown timers, fabricated scarcity, perpetual sales, or misleading deadlines. Overuse leads to customer fatigue and trust damage.

### CTA Placement in Video

**Mid-roll CTAs achieve highest conversion** at 16.95%. Place immediately after delivering biggest value or "a-ha moment" when reciprocity is highest.

**Placement strategy by length**: Short videos (under 30s)—end CTA only; Medium videos (30-90s)—mid-roll primary, end secondary; Long videos (90s+)—mid-roll primary, multiple secondary.

**Visual + verbal sync**: Always reinforce verbal CTA with on-screen text/button simultaneously since many viewers watch muted.

### Conversion Statistics for Reference

| Optimization | Impact |
|--------------|--------|
| First-person CTAs | +90% CTR |
| Personalized CTAs | +202% performance |
| Single CTA focus | +371% clicks |
| Action-oriented language | +121% conversion |
| Urgency elements (authentic) | Up to +332% |
| Trust signals near CTA | +133% conversion |
| Mobile-optimized CTA | +32.5% improvement |
